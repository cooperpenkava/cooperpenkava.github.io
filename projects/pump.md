_**Skills Used:**_
 - _**Failure Analysis, Root Cause Analysis**_
 - _**Systems Thinking - Analyzing Interconnectedness**_
 - _**Surface Microscopy - Optical, SEM**_
 - _**Materials Identification/Characterization - XRF, FTIR/TGA**_
 - _**Technical Documentation**_

For my Failure Analysis course, I performed an analysis on a hydraulic pump from a CAT Skid Loader that had stopped working completely. We took it completely apart (which itself was much more difficult than anticipated), analyzed every component visually, and took plenty of photos. From there, we built system diagrams explaining how each component worked together, how mass and energy was transferred through the system, and potential failure modes for these systems. Additionally, we used XRF and FTIR to identify the materials the pump was made out of and to examine any uncharacteristic spikes that may have indicated contamination of the oil. For the oil, we also used the TGA to see the oil's properties: we used the machine to characterize the small amount that was left in the pump and compared it to a test of new oil. We used optical microscopes to examine high wear surfaces and shards of metal we found around the pump. We also learned how to used the SEM for extremely high fidelity examination of the shards. Once we examined everything and came up with our theory of failure, we wrote a IEEE-format technical document surrounding our methodology, the contamination, likely root causes, and all conclusions, including former work and potential improvements to the system.
