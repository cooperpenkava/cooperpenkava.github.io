For my Principles of Integrated Engineering course, I created a 3D scanner from servo motors and a distance sensor across a two week sprint. On one hand, I like to think I went above and beyond, but on the other hand, I also sort of misunderstood the prompt of a "pan and tilt" scanner. I had a very exciting idea of using spherical coordinates to get a full model of an object, although all we really needed to do was scan the front face of an object at a distance. Luckily, the professors encouraged my enthusiasm, and pushed my partner and I to continue pursuing the idea. After I came up with theory behind what we needed to do to get spherical coordinates, my partner and I split up into arduino electrical/firmware and mechanical systems. I designed the system to always get a spherical coordinate based on the center plate, to rotate the object by 1 degree around the center axis (theta) and then move the sensor by 1 degree about the object (phi). Then, we got all of the readouts, translated them to a distance, and found rho from the sensor's total distance to the origin. I used the laser cutter and 3D printer mostly for all the pieces due to a need for speed in this two week sprint. I still wanted to make sure I understood what the Arduino was doing in the background, so I made sure I made the electrical diagram and helped with the equations for the spherical coordinates.
