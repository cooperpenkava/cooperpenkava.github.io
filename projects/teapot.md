For my Failure Analysis course, I analyzed the corrosion of a leaky teapot. I worked on a team of two. We received a leaky teapot that our professor was about to throw away. We analyzed the material of the teapot using XRF, then examined the joint between the top and bottom piece. We saw lots of corrosive discoloring, pitting, and more, so we mounted and etched a sample, viewing it on the optical microscope and SEM. We saw extreme pitting, so we looked further into the chemicals at play. The stainless steel was clearly very cheap metal, with a large concentration of manganese, and, after looking into Boston's water supply, we concluded that heat may have created conditions for chlorine to create pits at points of residual stress along the crimp joint.
